Care Plan Check

A clinician's assistant that reads the patient's chart and the open web, on two nodes, so that neither can leak into the other.
Team 4 · Brian Chen, Lynna Yang (physician), Kevin Yin · Track: Cluster & Scheduling · github.com/thebrianchen/careplan-fuzzball

The problem

Care plans fail for reasons that are not medical: no ride to dialysis, a copay the patient can't cover, a pantry that is closed on the only free day. The clues are spread across the visit, the chart and the neighborhood.

An AI that puts them together must read patient records and web pages. A web page can carry hidden instructions. In our tests, one agent holding both sent patient identifiers to an attacker in about one attack in four.

What we built

The clinician writes the plan. Care Plan Check reviews each item against what was said in the visit, what is in the chart (missed appointments, medication costs) and what is near the patient's home (bus schedules, pantries). It flags the items this patient probably can't follow, shows its evidence, and offers alternatives. The clinician accepts or dismisses each one.

Why it needs two nodes

Node 1 · patient sidePlanner: Nemotron 3.5 Super (120B)
Patient records on a team volume
Never reads a web page
asks only:
neighborhood,
kind of need, days→←records back
Node 2 · public sideWeb reader: Nemotron 3 Nano (30B)
Transit, pantry and clinic data
Never sees a patient
Fuzzball runs each side as its own service, with separate volumes and a signed caller identity on the one request that crosses. Storage is VAST.

The web reader can still be tricked by a poisoned page. It has nothing to give away.

Why it matters beyond healthcare: any assistant that must combine private records with untrusted web content has this problem. Splitting it across nodes, and letting the scheduler and storage enforce the split, is a pattern others can reuse.
